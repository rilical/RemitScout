# Quick primer
- The **mid-market rate** is your anchor; anything worse is a markup.
- **Total cost** = fee + FX spread vs. mid-market.
- Compare per corridor—rates and fees differ by route, method, and amount.

## What shapes the FX rate
- **Supply & demand**: Capital flows, trade balances, and sentiment move currencies.
- **Interest rates & inflation**: Central bank policy shifts FX relative values.
- **News & risk**: Geo events, data releases, and risk-on/off swings drive intraday moves.

## How providers price you
- **FX spread**: A markup over mid-market (e.g., 0.70% worse).
- **Transfer fees**: Flat or percentage, sometimes waived in promos.
- **Pay-in/payout**: Card vs. bank vs. cash changes the cost to serve.
- **Corridor risk/coverage**: Thin corridors can mean wider spreads.

## How to compare (in under a minute)
1) Start with the mid-market for your pair (e.g., USD/JOD).  
2) Get live quotes; note **received amount**.  
3) Compute **spread**: `(mid - quoted) / mid`.  
4) Add **fee** to see true total cost.  
5) Sort by **amount received**; flag speed and payout fit.

## When to send
- **Avoid weekends/holidays**: Wider spreads, slower settlement.
- **Watch cut-off times**: Late-day sends can slip a business day.
- **Ride or hedge moves**: If volatile, split sends or set alerts.

## Red flags to avoid
- “Zero fee” with a large FX haircut.
- Forced dynamic currency conversion (DCC) on cards.
- Opaque “estimated” payouts without showing rate + fee.

## Checklist before you send
- Confirm the **mid-market rate** today.
- Check **fee + spread** for your corridor and amount.
- Pick the **payout** that fits (bank = cheaper, cash = faster).
- Verify **KYC** needs so the transfer isn’t delayed.
- Save the quote if the provider allows it; rates move.

## FAQ
- **Is “no fee” always cheaper?** No. A higher FX spread can outweigh a waived fee.  
- **How often do rates change?** Continuously during market hours; thin liquidity widens spreads.  
- **Can I lock a rate?** Some providers offer rate holds; otherwise, send when the live rate looks good.
